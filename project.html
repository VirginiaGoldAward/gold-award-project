My Project

My project, “Back to School Beyond the Backpack” addresses a persistent challenge in our community: many schools don't provide free menstrual products directly in student bathrooms for easy access, instead they are only available out of the way in the nurses office. For my project I placed feminine products directly in student bathrooms, partnering with the National Beta service club to keep the stations stocked.

What Is a Gold Award Project?

The Girl Scout Gold Award is the highest service award a Girl Scout can receive. It is an internationally recognized honor. It requires substantial research, planning, and service as well as working with members of your community and an expert in the field of your issue. In order to receive the award girls must identify a root cause to their issue, linking it to a national or global problem. They must complete hours of research and planning to ensure their topic is worthwhile and solves an abundant problem. Girls must find a Project Advisor who is an expert in their subject matter. They must create a team of individuals from their community to help them with their project. They must keep careful track of their expenses, creating a budget and logging all expenditures and savings. Girls must complete at least 80 hours of work, teaching their communities and developing and expanding on their leadership skills along the way. In order to earn their award, they must complete their SMART goals, or milestones/markers set by the girls to prove their growth. Though it is an immense amount of work it is an incredibly worthwhile way to give back to your community.

General Information: What My Project Does and Why

My project places feminine products directly in every female student bathroom across my school. As an extension to this project I reached out to a younger Girl Scout troop to teach them about the importance of menstrual equity and how to create a similar movement at their school. I also created this website to teach people like you about the importance of this topic and how you too can start a movement in your community!

Audience: Students, educators, and school clubs

Core Deliverable: Product stations in each bathroom and implementation toolkit

Sustainability Plan: Partner with National Beta service club

Root Cause Analysis

The root cause of this issue is that schools lack a consistent system to support the needs of its students. By only having feminine products in the nurses office, which is all the way at the front of the school and very out of the way of students, girls often miss excessive amounts of instructional time seeking these products.  In many public places, menstrual products aren't treated as a basic health need like toilet paper or soap even though they should be. Lack of access to these essential products causes unnecessary stress, embarrassment and distractions during the school day. The inconsistent and unreliable access also creates major inequality among students.
